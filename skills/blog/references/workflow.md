### 1. Search strategy

Delegate this phase to `seo-keyword-research`. Determine one primary reader and one search intent: definition, how-to, comparison, evaluation, or another concrete job. Use current SERP/keyword data if available: actual result composition, competing pages, volume/difficulty, geography, language, collection date, and provider. Without measured data, analyze likely questions and available live pages qualitatively; volume, difficulty, ranking, and citation rate remain “not measured.” Inspect supplied references for argument, sources, structure, missing questions, and freshness.

Recommend a primary query, 3–5 supporting questions/terms, reader problem, thesis, content type, topic-based length range, and evidence gaps. Distinguish branded/entity queries from generic discovery intent. Present the strategy for the user's decision before locking it; existing authorization or a fully specified brief can satisfy this gate. Do not add keyword-density quotas, fake opportunity scores, or “citation guarantees.”

### 2. Claim and public-use gate

Build an evidence ledger before prose. Classify each material product, customer, market, or performance claim:

| Evidence state | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Externally verified | Cite a current primary/authoritative source and preserve scope. |
| Internally validated and approved | State sample, measurement window, denominator/method, limitations, and public-use approval. |
| Preliminary | Explicitly label the limited early evidence; do not generalize beyond it. |
| Hypothesis | Frame as a thesis, question, or proposed test. |
| Unavailable | Remove, replace with supportable wording, or retain a visible publication blocker. |

Record source, observation date, population, denominator/unit, time window, method, caveat, and public-use status. An external category statistic cannot prove this product's outcome. Correlation remains correlation. Private customer evidence or internal testing must not be relabeled as approved public customer proof. Do not reveal non-public scoring/recommendation recipes just to make the article detailed.

### 3. Discovery interview and outline

Delegate discovery/interview and outline to Editorial Draft, supplying the context, search strategy, ledger, and prior decisions. Fill six editorial needs: thesis, audience knowledge, supporting evidence, strongest counterargument, scope boundaries, and stakes. Ask 1–3 consequential questions at a time; skip answers already provided. A topic is not a thesis. When the user moves on with gaps, explicitly keep those gaps in review instead of padding the interview.

Outline each section with reader question/purpose, key argument, supporting evidence/source, approximate word budget, and optional supporting query only where natural. Put the direct answer near the opening when the query asks one. Choose a hook and closing that fit the promised outcome. Recommend known internal links and verified external sources, without inventing URLs. FAQs belong only when distinct recurring questions remain; schema only when it matches visible content and page type. Ask which thin/redundant section should be cut and offer a structural alternative if useful. Obtain the user's outline decision under the current workflow before drafting; do not require a redundant approval for an already approved outline.

Use `seo-content-refresher` to prepare the existing-article findings and revision plan before handing that plan to Editorial Draft. For existing-article review, preserve useful argument, approved claims, working links, and sections with observed performance. Identify stale facts, intent drift, unsupported statements, and gaps; show the proposed revision plan before rewriting. Interview/outline approval is needed only for a material change to thesis, audience, or approved scope. A working article is not a blank-page draft.

### 4. Draft or revise

Invoke Editorial Draft drafting or revision with the approved outline, preset, voice, and ledger. Work section by section internally to keep evidence and word budget attached to each argument; present sections separately when the user wants an interactive writing session, otherwise deliver one coherent draft after the agreed gates. Check each section's contribution to the thesis, transition, repetition, and source fidelity.

The title promise, H1, opening answer, and body must agree. Use primary search language where natural, often title/opening/relevant heading; adjacent repeated exact-match phrases and crawler-shaped headings require revision. Use definitions, tables, examples, and visual suggestions when they help the reader, not to manufacture extractable unsourced claims. Keep citations tied to the sentence they support. Annotated internal links/visuals remain working items until verified.

### 5. Editorial and confidentiality review

Execute Editorial Draft’s actual Hemingway, Hitchcock, Clarity, Voice, and Platform reviewers. Add Devil’s Advocate for disputed/strategic theses and Completeness for a guide, benchmark, methodology, or comparison. Follow the installed reviewer contracts, scoring, and revision loop; do not replace them with a generic checklist or simulated panel. Leave unavailable reviews pending. For an explicitly requested manual bypass, identify manual passes as manual, without independent panel receipts. Findings must quote a passage and include severity, reader consequence, and specific correction.

Check disclosure, public approval, private data, non-public methods, customer permissions, shipped-vs-hypothesis language, unsupported causality, and implementation detail unrelated to the reader's task. Resolve fatal and serious editorial/claim issues. Keep reviewer commentary and internal process residue outside public copy.

### 6. SEO and GEO checks

Delegate quality assessment to `seo-content-quality-auditor` and answer-readiness assessment to `seo-geo-content-optimizer`; retain their actual evidence-backed findings and unresolved dependencies. Check clarity, organization, referenceability, useful original evidence, credible experience/expertise, authority, and trust. These are inspection categories, not a tool-specific score. Audit direct answers, accurate definitions, headings, natural query language, verified links, useful comparison tables, dates/methods, accurate entity naming, metadata, and schema matching visible content. Never add repetitive FAQs, fake first-person experience, or unsupported “quotable” claims to fill a benchmark.

When a quality/SEO tool actually runs, report its real check/result, date, and coverage. Without it, mark “not run” and list the manual checks. Auto-fix mechanical issues; surface changes to thesis, tone, evidence scope, or public claims for the user's decision. Do not promise rankings, citations, or a search engine's preference without actual supporting evidence.

### 7. Polish and handoff

Run Editorial Draft’s canonical AI-pattern scrub and final line edit, preserving approved voice. Default to active verbs, short paragraphs, sentence case, concrete details, varied rhythm, zero em-dashes, and no “leverage,” “transformative,” “seamless,” hype, or negative parallelism. Preserve accurate quotations and technical terminology. Cut repetition, padded transitions, meta commentary, fake depth, and a closing that only repeats the article.

Recheck the ledger after polishing: a line edit must not turn preliminary evidence into a settled result. Verify actual links or mark pending. Save the article and internal package after the applicable outline/revision gate. Report word count and reading-time estimate with its assumption. Publication, CMS edits, spreadsheet logging, and moving into a published directory are separate authorized actions. Track targeting only in an existing approved canonical tracker; do not create an invisible parallel source of truth.

## Worked example

Fictional illustration only. A proposed article is “How to review an account import before applying it.” There is no keyword tool and no product outcome study.

**Strategy:** Informational how-to for an operations lead. Volume/difficulty: not measured. Primary query is a qualitative recommendation, not a measured ranking opportunity.
**Thesis:** Review conflicts and expected record changes before applying a representative import. `[FICTIONAL EDITORIAL THESIS]`
**Outline:** Define the change → inspect conflicts → compare expected result → decide whether to apply → recover if verification fails.
**Claim ledger:** “This reduces import time by 40%” is unavailable and removed. A generic example using invented rows is labeled fictional. Any claimed product preview capability needs a verified source.

Weak: “Our seamless workflow revolutionizes imports and guarantees better results.” Better: “The example checks duplicate IDs before applying the file.” The example does not establish a product feature or measured benefit.

## Quality checks

- Strategy and outline decisions are satisfied without repeated unnecessary gates.
- Each material claim has an evidence state, scope, source, and public-use treatment.
- The article delivers the title promise, reader task, counterpoint, and useful examples.
- Actual tool values are separated from qualitative analysis and manual checks.
- SEO/GEO structure improves comprehension; no keyword stuffing or unsupported extractable claims.
- Fatal/serious issues and public approval gaps are resolved or explicit blockers; final wording matches evidence strength.

## Anti-patterns

Invented volumes/audit scores; a topic with no thesis; cloned competitor structure without a point of view; forced FAQs/schema; publicly exposing private recipes; invented customer quotes; third-party stats used as product ROI; article replacement instead of scoped refresh; first-person origin stories for company copy without approval; simulated independent review; automatic publishing.
