# What matters to us — EXAMPLE

This is the file `cluster.relevance_context_file` points at. The cluster
and brief steps read it to judge whether a group of signals matters to you
before it becomes a theme. The first `/content-engine` run writes your own
copy, `context.md`, from your setup answers.

Nothing here needs to be true for your product — it's a worked example for
a fictional CI-flakiness-dashboard product from Example Corp, shaped like a real
one so you can see the kind of content that's useful here, then replace it
with your own.

## What we build

Example Corp's dashboard flags flaky tests in your CI pipeline before
they waste a team's afternoon re-running builds.

## Who we're writing for

- **Practitioner** — an engineer tired of re-running a build because a test
  failed for no reason. Cares about: does this actually fix the problem,
  how much setup does it take.
- **Engineering manager** — owns CI reliability as a team metric. Cares
  about: can they point to a number that improved, not just a vibe.

## What's actually relevant to us

- Flaky test detection, root-causing, and quarantine strategies.
- CI pipeline reliability as a measurable metric (not just anecdotes).
- Build observability tooling — anything that makes a CI failure legible
  instead of a black box.

## What's NOT relevant, even if it's popular

- General AI-coding-assistant discourse unless it specifically touches
  test generation or CI — that's a different theme than ours.
- Pure DevOps/infra content with no testing angle.
